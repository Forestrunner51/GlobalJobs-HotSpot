Apply quickly

  1. Add your resume above. It's read on this device: it fills your answer bank and shows how well each posting fits.
  2. ☆ Save the postings you want (or press s).
  3. Press Apply. The employer's form opens in a new tab and your answer bank waits here with a Copy button per answer. Submit the form yourself; when you come back, say whether you applied.

        About the data

        How current it is

        Survey estimates from May 2025, published in 2026. Not live job-market data.

        Concentration is not opportunity

        The location quotient (LQ) compares a job's share of local employment with its share nationally. An LQ of 1.0 is average and 3.0 means three times as common as usual. A high LQ means the job is unusually concentrated in a place. It does not mean employers there are hiring, or that moving there is a good idea. Check job postings, cost of living and wages before deciding anything.

        By default, metros with fewer than 50 jobs in an occupation are left out of LQ rankings, so a handful of jobs in a small place can't top the list. "Number of jobs" answers a different question: where the most people do this work.

        Missing is not zero

        BLS withholds estimates that are unreliable or could identify an employer. Those metros show as no data: a flat grey dot on the globe, a dashed ring on the map, and a separate list under the table. They never count as low values. Where BLS published jobs but not an LQ, we calculate one from its employment totals and mark it with †.

        Your resume

        Resume files are read with your browser's file API and processed by code served from this site. No network request carries resume text, and the page's Content Security Policy only allows connections to this site's own static files. Matching is keyword-based and often wrong. Treat the suggestions as a starting point.

        Coverage

        US metropolitan statistical areas only. Rural and nonmetro areas, and all countries outside the US, are not included yet.

        Job postings

        The postings tab lists live openings that mention the programming languages in jobs/sources.json (Rust, C++, Go, Python and others). They come from the public job-board APIs of the companies in jobs/sources.json (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby), the latest Hacker News "Who is hiring?" thread and, if you add API keys, Adzuna and USAJobs. Cloud and infrastructure roles (Cloud / AWS, Platform, Infrastructure, SRE, DevOps) are found by job title, whatever languages the posting lists, and have one-tap Quick search buttons above the list. Frameworks and cloud tools (React, Spring Boot, AWS, ...) are tags on those postings, matched in the same text; a posting that mentions only a framework isn't included. A posting can appear because it lists a language once among several; "Focused postings only" hides those. Postings that only say "Remote" or name a city outside the US are in the list but not on the map. Hybrid comes from the location field or phrases like "3 days a week in the office"; On-site means a posting names a place and mentions neither, so some of those are really hybrid. Postings refresh automatically: closed roles drop out of company boards, and feed postings older than 60 days are removed. Pay ranges come from the board when it provides one, otherwise from the first annual range written in the posting. Applying always happens on the employer's own site.

        Applying

        Nothing on this site applies for you. Save postings, prepare tailored materials with apply/jobprep.py, then fill in and submit each application yourself on the employer's site. The answer bank only puts your own saved answers on the clipboard. Your saved postings, statuses and answer bank stay in this browser's storage and are never sent anywhere. Adding a resume on the postings tab reads it on your device and keeps only the list of skills it mentions and your contact details, in this browser; the resume itself isn't stored. "Send my list to another device" puts your list inside a link (after the #, which browsers don't send to any server); whoever opens that link gets the list, so send it only to yourself.
